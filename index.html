why it's hard

Not one label. Many, at once.

In the Mechanisms of Action setting, a single compound can trigger several biological mechanisms simultaneously. The model can't pick one class — it has to emit a calibrated probability for every one of the 206 targets, for every treatment.

That, layered on top of noisy and heavily correlated high-dimensional signal, is what makes the problem interesting. Most of the work happens before the model: reshaping raw measurements into a form the network can actually learn from.

  • 01

    Multi-label by nature

    One treatment may map to many mechanisms — the output is a vector of probabilities, not a class.

  • 02

    High dimensional

    Hundreds of gene and cell features, many carrying overlapping information.

  • 03

    Sparse, uneven targets

    Some mechanisms are rare, so naïve validation splits quietly mislead you.

  • 04

    Noisy signal

    Biological measurements are skewed with outliers that destabilize training.

the pipeline

Eight stages, raw signal to ranked prediction.

The performance comes from the whole workflow, not one model. Every stage below is shown with the real code from the notebooks — expand any block to read it.

Phase 1 — Signal shaping

before the model sees anything
1

RankGauss normalization

sklearn · QuantileTransformer

Raw biological measurements are skewed with extreme values. A quantile transform maps every gene and cell feature onto a clean normal distribution.

  • Tames outliers feature-by-feature
  • Stabilizes neural-network optimization
  • Fit on train, applied identically to test
2

PCA compression

sklearn · PCA

Genes and cells move together, so the raw space is bigger than the useful one. PCA adds compressed summaries of the strongest patterns — a second view alongside the originals.

  • Separate decompositions for genes and cells
  • Fit on train + test concatenated
  • Augments, doesn't replace, raw features
3

Statistical feature engineering

pandas

Aggregate descriptors capture the overall shape of a response — two compounds that don't match column-by-column may still share a higher-level profile.

  • Sums, means, std, skew, kurtosis per group
  • Products of correlated cell pairs
  • Squared versions of key features
4

Cluster features

sklearn · KMeans

Grouping samples with similar gene, cell, or PCA profiles — then one-hot encoding the cluster id back in — gives the model context about which response family a sample belongs to.

  • Clusters over genes, cells, and PCA space
  • One-hot encoded as new inputs
5

Variance filtering

sklearn · VarianceThreshold

Features that barely move add noise, not signal. A variance threshold drops the dead weight before training to keep the feature space focused and reduce overfitting.

Phase 2 — Modeling & ensembling

learning the mechanisms
6

Network architecture

PyTorch

A weight-normalized MLP with batch-norm and dropout learns non-linear relationships across the engineered features, emitting one logit per target. A recalibration guard keeps weight-norm stable.

  • BatchNorm → weight-norm dense → LeakyReLU
  • Label-smoothed BCE loss
  • NaN recalibration on the weight vectors
7

Stratified cross-validation

iterative-stratification

Because targets are sparse and uneven, folds are built with multi-label stratification so label balance is preserved across train and validation — honest estimates on unseen data.

8

Training & ensembling

PyTorch · OneCycleLR

Each fold trains with a one-cycle schedule and early stopping, saving the best checkpoint. Final predictions blend multiple model families — they make different mistakes, so the blend is steadier than any one.

  • Adam + OneCycleLR, best-checkpoint per fold
  • Averaged across folds, seeds, and architectures
